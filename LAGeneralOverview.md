## 🔥 MEASURE FD — Fire Department Sales Tax
**Initiative Ordinance | Majority to pass | Effective April 1, 2027**

A permanent 0.5% sales tax (10.25% → **10.75%**) generating **~$345 million/year** exclusively for LAFD. No sunset — ends only by voter repeal.

**What the money funds:**
- 🧑‍🚒 Hiring firefighters, paramedics & staff
- 🚒 Purchasing & maintaining vehicles and equipment
- 🏗️ Building & upgrading fire stations
- 🌲 Brush clearance

**Cost:** ~$86/person/year · ~$345/family of 4/year

**Oversight:** Annual public audits + 5-member Citizens' Oversight Committee (one appointment each from the Mayor, Fire Chief, City Controller, City Administrative Officer, and Council Public Safety Committee Chair).

### 🛡️ The Maintenance-of-Effort Clause
A built-in safeguard many voters overlook: **the City must continue funding LAFD at no less than its average share of the discretionary budget over the 10 fiscal years before the tax takes effect.** If the City budgets LAFD below that baseline in any year, **it cannot collect the Measure FD tax that year.** The revenue is designed to *supplement*, not *replace*, existing funding.

### ⚠️ The Overtime Pension Question
In late October, a separate Council motion proposed pension credits for involuntary LAFD overtime. UFLAC states firmly: *"There has never been a proposal or even a discussion to make regular overtime shifts pensionable."* The pension proposal would require its own charter amendment, actuarial study, and separate voter approval — potentially in 2028. The two are legally independent, but the *timing* has raised questions among opponents about where FD revenue ultimately flows.

### ⚖️ Both Sides

**YES:** The last major LAFD investment was Prop F in 2000 — 26 years ago. The city has grown, call volume has grown, and the MOE clause prevents the City from using this tax as an excuse to cut existing funding. Every dollar is restricted to LAFD by law.

**NO:** This is the third local sales tax increase in two years, pushing LA to 10.75% — among the highest in the nation. The sales tax is regressive, hitting lower-income residents hardest. The Daily News argues "generous compensation packages and rising pension obligations" guarantee "at least some of the benefits of a new tax will inevitably be absorbed by union deals."

## 🏗️ MEASURE LA — City Infrastructure, Budget, Finance & Contracting
**Charter Amendment | Majority to pass**

Five structural changes. No new tax. No new revenue.

| # | Change | Effect |
|---|--------|--------|
| 1 | **Capital Infrastructure Plan** | City must adopt and maintain a long-term plan for roads, water, transit, etc. |
| 2 | **Two-Year Budget Cycle** | Shifts from annual to biennial budgeting |
| 3 | **Stronger DPW Director** | Expands authority; allows elimination or restructuring of the Board of Public Works |
| 4 | **Remove commercial/mortgage bars** | City may engage in business enterprises and mortgage city-owned property (currently prohibited) |
| 5 | **Contracting rules** | Revises procurement procedures |

### Endorsements

| ✅ YES | ❌ NO |
|--------|-------|
| La Defensa | LA County Democratic Party |
| | Los Angeles Daily News |
| | Thrive LA |

### ⚖️ Both Sides

**YES:** The city needs flexibility to compete for billion-dollar infrastructure projects. The current charter restrictions are relics that prevent LA from operating like peer cities.

**NO:** Removing the bar on mortgaging city property and entering commercial ventures is a structural change that reduces long-term accountability. A two-year budget cycle means voters get half as many annual checkpoints. The LACDP's "No" is notable — this isn't a simple partisan split.

## 🌳 MEASURE PRK — Parks Budget Doubled
**Charter Amendment | Majority to pass | No new tax**

Doubles the charter-mandated *minimum* General Fund allocation to Recreation & Parks, phased in over 10 years.

| | Current | Full (FY 2036–37) |
|--|---------|-------------------|
| Minimum allocation | 0.0325% of assessed value (~$303M/yr) | 0.065% of assessed value (~$986–995M/yr) |
| New tax? | — | **No.** Reallocates existing General Fund. |
| Emergency valve | — | Council may cut up to 30% in a declared fiscal emergency, but never below today's level. |

**Why now:** The current 0.0325% formula dates to **1937**. **Proposition K** (a 0.1% property tax override) **expires in FY 2026–27**, creating a funding cliff. PRK raises the floor to partially offset that loss. The parks system carries an estimated $15 billion in capital backlog and full-time staff have been cut ~30% since 2009.

| City Controller Kenneth Mejia | |
| LA League of Conservation Voters | |
| League of Women Voters of Greater LA | |
| Los Angeles Neighborhood Land Trust | |

### ⚖️ Both Sides

**YES:** The 1937 formula is an anachronism. This locks in a funding floor so no future Council can starve the department. The Prop K expiration makes this urgent. Parks are critical infrastructure, especially for low-income communities.

**NO:** The Daily News argues the Council should fix the budget through normal appropriations, not amend the charter. The 10-year phase-in means the full benefit won't arrive until 2036–37. The city also charges parks for utilities, which opponents argue artificially inflates the department's costs.

## ⚖️ MEASURE EE — Ethics, Elections & Governance
**Charter Amendment | Majority to pass | 24 charter sections changed | Council vote: 12–0**

| Topic | Today | If EE Passes |
|-------|-------|--------------|
| 🏛️ Council meetings | ≥ 3 days/week | ≥ **1 day/week** (special meetings still allowed) |
| 📝 Referendum threshold | 10% of last mayoral vote (~93,000) | **15%** (~139,500) |
| 💰 Campaign finance penalty | Up to $2,000/violation | Greater of **$15,000** or **3× the violation** |
| 🚫 Ethics Commission post-service ban | 2 years, limited scope | **5 years**, any City or LAUSD board office |
| 👮 Police Inspector General | Police Commission *can* stop an investigation | Police Commission **cannot** |
| 🚒 Fire Independent Assessor | Fire Commission *can* stop an audit | Fire Commission **cannot** |
| ⏱️ Council veto review | 21 days | **15 calendar days** (21 for Police Chief removal) |
| 🏘️ Neighborhood Councils | Open to residents, workers, property owners | Open to "all stakeholders, **as defined by ordinance**" |
| 🗣️ Language access | Not in charter | **Added** to Standards of Service |

### Endorsements

| ✅ YES | ❌ NO |
|--------|-------|
| La Defensa | LA County Democratic Party |
| Knock LA | Los Angeles Daily News |
| DSA-LA | Councilmember Monica Rodriguez (CD7) |
| CAIR-LA | |
| Councilmembers Tim McOsker, Katy Yaroslavsky, Eunisses Hernandez, Marqueece Harris-Dawson | |
| Diego Andrades | |

### ⚖️ Both Sides

**YES:** The Police Commission should not be able to shut down an Inspector General investigation — that's a critical accountability fix. The $15K penalty is long overdue ($2,000 is a cost of doing business). The 5-year cooling-off period prevents revolve-door politics. The Council can still hold special meetings, so "weekly" is a *minimum*, not a cap.

**NO:** Going from 3 meetings/week to 1 reduces public access to the Council. Raising the referendum threshold from 10% to 15% makes it 50% harder for voters to overturn Council actions. The Neighborhood Councils change removes a charter-level guarantee of participation and replaces it with an ordinance-defined standard the Council could narrow.

### 📌 Key Context
- This is a **bundle** — you can't vote for the oversight changes without the meeting reduction.
- The 12–0 Council vote shows strong bipartisan support among Councilmembers, but the LACDP's "No" indicates the party base may disagree with its own elected officials.
- CAIR-LA supports EE specifically because it aligns City ethics rules with **LA County Measure E** (the County Ethics Commission independence measure also on this ballot).

## 📐 MEASURE PL — Planning Department
**Charter Amendment | Majority to pass | Council vote: 13–0 (General Plan item: 8–7)**

| Change | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| 🏛️ 7 Area Commissions → 1 Appeals Commission | 35 local members → minimum 7 citywide members (de novo review) |
| ⏰ Hard deadlines | Planning boards: **30 days** on remand. Council: **21 days** for final vote. |
| 🏢 Quasi-judicial authority | Planning Department gains authority over all quasi-judicial applications (variances, CUPs, zone changes) |
| 📜 Appeal rights → ordinance | Appeal rights for variances, CUPs, zone changes, and GPAs removed from charter → governed by **ordinance** |
| 📏 Floor area rules | Council may change floor-area and buildable-area regulations by **ordinance** |

### ⚠️ The Automatic-Approval Flip
The single most consequential change: **Currently, if the Council doesn't act on a General Plan Amendment within 75 days of the Planning Commission and Mayor's joint approval, the amendment is automatically *denied*. Under PL, it would be automatically *approved*.**

This inverts the default. Inaction becomes approval rather than rejection. Proponents say it eliminates foot-dragging. Opponents say it creates a perverse incentive where gridlock becomes a rubber stamp.

### Endorsements

| ✅ YES | ❌ NO |
|--------|-------|
| Los Angeles Daily News | LA County Democratic Party |
| | La Defensa |

### ⚖️ Both Sides

**YES:** The current system kicks projects back and forth between commissions and Council indefinitely. Hard deadlines and a single appeals body will actually get housing and infrastructure built. The Daily News — the only major outlet endorsing a charter amendment this cycle — calls it necessary modernization.

**NO:** Moving appeal rights from the charter to ordinance means the Council can weaken or eliminate them with a simple vote — no voter approval needed. The automatic-approval flip is a significant shift in the balance of power. Reducing 35 area commissioners to a minimum of 7 citywide members raises questions about local representation. La Defensa's specific concern: the ordinance shift "makes appeal rights easier for the Council to weaken or eliminate."

## 🎓 MEASURE SC — School Board Campaign Finance
**Charter Amendment | Majority to pass**

A **technical cleanup** that removes redundant and sometimes conflicting charter language about LAUSD Board of Education campaign finance. **No dollar limits change. No new rules.** It clarifies that LAUSD Board candidates follow the identical contribution limits, disclosure requirements, and penalties as City mayoral candidates.

## 🏚️ MEASURE TE — Fire Victim Transfer Tax Exemption
**Ordinance | Majority to pass**

A **one-time** exemption from the Measure ULA "mansion tax" for residential properties **damaged or destroyed in the Palisades Fire** and sold between **January 7, 2025 and January 6, 2030**.

| Detail | Value |
|--------|-------|
| What's waived | Measure ULA transfer tax (applies to properties over $5M, as adjusted for inflation) |
| Who qualifies | Owner of record on the date the fire began; property damaged/destroyed in the Palisades Fire |
| Window | Jan 7, 2025 – Jan 6, 2030 (5 years) |
| Scope | One-time, per property |
| Owner-occupancy required? | **No** |
| Ongoing fiscal impact? | **No** |

### ⚖️ Both Sides

**YES:** Families are rebuilding after a catastrophic disaster. This removes a tax barrier from an already traumatic process. It's one-time, targeted, and has no ongoing fiscal impact. Park: "The least we can do."
